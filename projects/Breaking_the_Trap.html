圈套：留学中被侵犯的“羔羊”

A Chinese student in Britain describes alleged assault, coercion and her decision to speak publicly.

01

The violence that changed her understanding

In January 2024, Gao Yuan was in a Chinese restaurant in Britain with a man she had been seeing for just over two months. According to her account in the report, she confronted him near the toilets about his contact with other women. She says he acknowledged it and challenged her to do anything about it.

Gao began recording on her phone and asked him to repeat himself to the camera. She describes him trying to take the device, pushing her to the floor, shutting the door and striking her. She says the assault lasted close to three minutes while she called for help and held onto the recording phone.

The restaurant owner heard the cries and came to the door. Gao’s account describes the woman forcing her way in and helping her leave. Gao took her phone and went upstairs to call a taxi.

“The tears just kept falling,” she recalls. In the taxi, the restaurant owner called her by video and pointed out blood around her eye and on her face. Gao decided to contact police and returned to the restaurant. She says officers later accompanied her to hospital, where she waited more than five hours to be seen.

After returning home, she received a photograph from the man showing redness on his neck. He said she had injured him. Gao interpreted this as an attempt to portray the encounter as mutual violence. The following day, she says he came to her home, knocked and shouted for her to delete the video. She and her flatmate remained silent until he left. She contacted police again to report what she described as stalking and harassment.

When he threatened legal action, Gao began reconsidering the relationship as a whole.

02

How a relationship began

The report places their first meeting on 18 November of the previous year, at a friend’s birthday party. It describes the man as a Chinese professor who had lived in Britain for more than 20 years. Gao says he repeatedly encouraged her to drink, put an arm around her shoulders and offered help with her life as a student.

At the end of the gathering, Gao says she was heavily intoxicated. He offered to arrange a taxi and take her home. She initially said a friend would accompany her, but that friend was helping another person. Gao recalls getting into the taxi and later realising she was somewhere unfamiliar.

Gao alleges that she was sexually assaulted that night. She says she had not wanted sexual contact and initially understood what happened as rape. According to her account, the man subsequently visited her university, said he loved her and wanted to marry her, and introduced her to others as his girlfriend.

She describes struggling with whether his attention and apparent willingness to look after her might let her put the first night behind her. She eventually agreed to see him. During the relationship, she says he belittled her, told her that she needed him and emphasised his influence in Britain. Later, she experienced him becoming emotionally distant.

Gao says she saw messages on his phone that led her to believe similar behaviour was occurring with other women. The report presents her interpretation of a pattern: intoxication, an alleged assault, promises of a relationship and then withdrawal. Those allegations are not a judicial finding established by this supplied document.

She consulted a lawyer and recounted the sequence from their first meeting to the restaurant incident. The article reports the lawyer suggesting that the account could involve manipulation related to sexual assault. It does not provide the underlying legal advice or a final case determination.

03

Finding a way to speak

In the month after the restaurant incident, Gao says she received threats from the man and people associated with him, warning her not to make the matter public. She feared for her safety. Friends suggested using her existing social-media presence to post updates regularly, so an unexplained silence might prompt concern.

On 8 February 2024, approximately a month after the assault she describes, she posted a Douyin video publicly recounting her experience for the first time. The report says versions posted to other platforms were repeatedly restricted for content involving violence, privacy or sexual material. Gao edited names and obscured images of injuries in response.

The report states that videos about her experience later received a combined 60–70 million views. It also describes her inbox becoming a place where others shared accounts of their own experiences and says more than 170 people contacted her on Rednote. These are figures reported in the article; independent analytics are not supplied here.

The article includes accounts relayed by Gao from a remote-course student and a former intern, describing messages, drinking invitations and behaviour that they considered inappropriate. The supplied document does not establish those accounts through court records or a response from the accused.

Police evidence-gathering and replying to messages disrupted Gao’s everyday life. She nevertheless wanted others to know what she said had happened. The article closes by anticipating a court hearing the following January. It does not supply a later outcome.
