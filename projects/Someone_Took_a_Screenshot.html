01 / Literature review

Privacy is a boundary
we manage together.

Screenshots turn fleeting conversations into durable, portable objects. Three perspectives help explain what a notification changes.

Screenshot practices

From capture to circulation

A screenshot can be a memory, evidence or social currency. Taking it and sharing it are distinct acts; its journey can extend beyond the original conversation.

Jaynes, 2020; Hu, 2020 · 截屏传播
Privacy boundaries

Shared information, different rules

A sender and recipient may disagree about what can be saved or passed on. Communication privacy management directs attention to these negotiated boundaries.

Communication privacy management · 隐私边界管理
Privacy calculus

Risk competes with connection

People can value privacy while continuing to disclose. Social rewards, emotion and perceived control all influence how they balance risks and benefits.

Gerber et al., 2018; Trepte et al., 2020 · 隐私计算
The research focus

When a previously invisible action becomes visible, how do people renegotiate what they share—and whom they trust?

04 / Analysis

Visibility empowers.
It can also unsettle.

Explore three themes and the participant accounts behind them.

Two orientations identified in the thesis

Active defence / 主动防御型

Control what leaves in the first place.

Filter sensitive details before sending. A notification reinforces awareness, but protection depends partly on selective disclosure.

Relationship trust / 关系信任型

Rely on who is receiving it.

Trust familiar contacts and restrict what strangers can see. Technical safeguards may matter less than the relationship.

Analytical orientations, not fixed personality types or quantified segments. Main thesis pp. 10–12.

05 / Relationships

The same alert.
A different social meaning.

Relationship, power and the meaning of a setting shape how people respond. Select a context to explore the interview evidence.

Contextual synthesis of participant accounts, not an experimental comparison or a prediction of every user’s response.

06 / Design implications

Make the boundary clear.
Make the choice meaningful.

The thesis translates interview insights into three design directions.

01 / Explain first

Tell users before they discover it by accident.

Explain when the feature applies, who sees the notice and what it cannot prevent. Make changes to the rules understandable.

Responds to gaps in feature awareness · pp. 24–25, 28–29
02 / Offer context

Different content needs different boundaries.

Explore permissions based on message sensitivity and relationships, including requests before capture rather than notices only afterwards.

Responds to different privacy needs · pp. 26–27
03 / Support reflection

Help people pause before private details travel.

Consider timely risk prompts and sensitive-information masking. Support respectful sharing without implying complete protection.

Responds to source-control strategies · pp. 26, 28
These are research-informed proposals. The thesis did not test their usability, technical feasibility or effects on privacy outcomes.
07 / Conclusion

Knowing is a starting point.

A notification can make a boundary visible. Respecting and enforcing it remains a social—and technical—challenge.

Awareness

The hidden act becomes visible.

Users can question capture, adjust expectations and reconsider what they disclose.

Behaviour

People adapt in different ways.

Accounts include restraint, explanations, alternative recording practices and changes of channel.

Conditions

Protection depends on context.

Trust, awareness, power and the limits of detection shape what the feature can accomplish.

08 / Discussion

A privacy feature is also
a relationship signal.

The findings connect interface design with everyday negotiations over trust and information.

Awareness and control need separate treatment

Participants value knowing that a capture occurred. Yet knowing does not remove the image or trace its onward circulation. Clear risk communication needs to distinguish perceived control from effective protection.

Power determines whether a boundary can be defended

The ability to object to a screenshot depends on the relationship. A notice can offer useful information while leaving a student, employee or younger family member unable to challenge the other person.

What the evidence can establish

Fifteen qualitative interviews provide insight into interpretations and reported strategies. The sample is concentrated among relatively highly educated young people. Retrospective self-reports cannot establish causal behavioural effects, population prevalence or the security of any current platform.

What future work could examine

The thesis proposes combining interviews with behavioural logs or eye-tracking, comparing age and occupational contexts, and examining how platform governance and responsibility for secondary circulation can support privacy.

Discussion draws on the thesis’s analysis and concluding reflection, pp. 30–31. Platform behaviour described in the study belongs to its 2025 context.
